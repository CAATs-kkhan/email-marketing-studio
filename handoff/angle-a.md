# Angle A verdict: `revenue leakage help` (outcome-led)

Compared with the control `remote data analysis`, for email 01. Judged only from `.claude/skills/cold-email/references/subject-lines.md` and `brand/voice.md`. No open or reply rates are claimed; none exist.

## Rule check
- Lowercase, 3 words, 21 characters: passes the 50-character limit and the "2-4 words, lowercase" rule in the voice file.
- No numbers, urgency words, punctuation, emojis, first name or spam triggers ("free", "guarantee", "act now").

## Against the control
| | Control: `remote data analysis` | Angle A: `revenue leakage help` |
|---|---|---|
| What it signals | A service category. Reads as a vendor offer. | A specific problem. Reads closer to a colleague's note. |
| Guidance fit | Plain and boring (good), but "pitching product in subject" applies since it names the service. | Contextual pain point, which the guidance says works. "help" is mild, but it still points at an offer. |
| Risk | Low. | Medium (see below). |

## By audience
- **Agency owners (suits Angle A better).** Revenue leakage is a tax and customs problem their public-sector or finance clients may have. The subject names a client problem they could win work on, which matches the voice file's line for owners ("how this helps them fill or win client work"). The control gives them less reason to open.
- **HR recruiters (suits Angle A less).** Recruiters think in roles and skills, not leakage. "revenue leakage" may read as irrelevant to their open requisitions, so it may be filtered. The control's "remote" and "data analysis" map directly to a role or project, which is what the voice file says to give recruiters.

## Risks
- **Overclaim.** The voice file says "revenue leakage" is not named in the CV. The subject sounds like a promise of finding leakage. The body must keep the approved phrasing (analysis of tax and customs data to find revenue leakage) and state no recovered amounts. A subject that promises help the body only partly backs is a mismatch.
- **Salesy flavour.** "help" is soft, but it is a service word. It is not on the guidance's avoid list, though it comes nearer to "pitching" than a neutral subject would.
- **Spam wording.** None found.
- **Relevance gap.** Leakage may mean nothing to a recruiter, with no CV-backed outcome to explain it in the subject.

## Recommendation
Do not replace the control for everyone. Use `revenue leakage help` for the agency-owner versions only, and keep `remote data analysis` for HR recruiters. If one subject must cover both audiences, keep the control. Whichever is used, make sure email 01's body states the revenue-leakage line in the approved wording.
