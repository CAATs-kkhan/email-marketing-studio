# Angle B verdict: `remote acl analyst` (credential-led)

Compared with the control `remote data analysis`, for email 01 (the intro).
Basis: `.claude/skills/cold-email/references/subject-lines.md` and `brand/voice.md` only. No send data exists, so this is reasoning, not measurement. No open or reply rates are claimed.

## Rule check
- Lowercase, 3 words, 18 characters. Fits the 50-character limit and the 2-4 word, lowercase guidance. The control fits too (3 words, 20 characters).
- No numbers, urgency words, salesy words ("increase", "boost", "ROI"), punctuation or spam triggers.
- No em dashes, no name in the subject.
- The preview line may need adjusting. Keep it at 90 characters or fewer.

## B vs the control
| | Control: `remote data analysis` | B: `remote acl analyst` |
|---|---|---|
| What it names | The service | A tool plus a role |
| Reads like | A service description, close to a vendor pitch | A job title or a candidate profile |
| Jargon | None | "acl", which only audit and finance people recognize |
| "Pitching product in subject" risk (guidance: -57% replies) | Present, mild | Present, mild and slightly lower, since it names a role rather than an offer |

Neither subject uses the "internal camouflage" examples in the guidance (a topic a colleague would raise, like "hiring ops" or "employee turnover"). Both announce the sender's skill set rather than a topic the reader cares about, so B is not a clear step up on that principle.

## By audience
**HR recruiters.** B is weaker than the control for most of them. "acl" is a niche tool name. A recruiter who does not know it gets less meaning from B than from "data analysis", which they can place at once. The word "analyst" does help, because it matches the role titles they search and fill. Net: the control is clearer here.

**Agency owners.** B suits them better. An owner whose agency places audit, finance or public-sector analytics talent is likely to know ACL, and the tool name signals a specific, scarce skill. That fits the voice file's line for owners about helping fill or win client work. Net: B is the stronger of the two for owners who serve audit and finance clients, and no better for owners who do not.

## Risks
- **Spam or overclaim wording:** none. ACL is a listed tool in the voice file, and "analyst" matches the sender's title, so the subject claims nothing unconfirmed. It has no "certified" or "expert" wording, so no ACDA wording issue.
- **Ambiguity:** "acl" is an acronym with other meanings (for example access control list in IT), so a reader outside audit may misread it. The email body must name ACL data analysis early to settle this.
- **Candidate-pitch feel:** "remote ... analyst" can read as a job seeker's subject line. For recruiters this may be filed as a CV submission rather than an offer of work done on their behalf.

## Recommendation
Do not replace the control with B. Keep `remote data analysis` as the default for email 01, and keep `remote acl analyst` only as a variant to try with agency owners.
